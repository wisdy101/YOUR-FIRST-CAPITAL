Questions founders ask before raising money

How can I raise money for my startup?

Start by defining how much you need, what the money will fund and which milestone it should help you reach. Then identify potential backers, prepare a clear explanation of your business, make a specific ask and track each conversation. Your First Capital provides a structured way to work through these steps.

How do I ask friends and family to invest in my business?

Be direct, prepared and honest about the opportunity and the risks. Explain what your business does, how much you are seeking, what the money will be used for and what arrangement you are proposing. Give people room to decline without pressure, and get appropriate professional advice before agreeing to terms.

Do I need a venture capitalist to start a business?

No. Venture capital is one possible funding source, but it is not right for every business or every stage. Depending on your circumstances, you may explore personal savings, revenue, grants, loans, angel investors or support from your existing network. Choose a route that fits your business and the obligations you can reasonably take on.

How much startup capital should I raise?

Work backwards from the next meaningful milestone. Estimate the costs required to reach it, explain your assumptions and avoid choosing a number simply because it sounds impressive. Your target should be connected to a clear use of funds and a realistic plan.

Should I take a loan or give investors equity?

It depends on your cash flow, growth plans, risk tolerance and the terms available. A loan generally creates a repayment obligation; equity usually means sharing ownership and potentially future upside. Other structures may have different legal and financial consequences. Understand the terms and seek qualified advice before accepting money.

What if nobody in my network can fund me?

Your network can help with more than money: introductions, feedback, early customers and credibility. If direct funding is not a fit, use conversations to learn, ask for relevant introductions and assess other options such as grants, angel investors, accelerators, revenue or suitable lending. Do not pressure people to invest money they cannot afford to lose.

Will this guide guarantee that I raise money in 30 days?

No. Fundraising depends on your business, the strength of your proposal, the people you approach, market conditions and many factors outside your control. The 30-day plan is designed to help you prepare and take consistent action; it cannot guarantee investor interest or funding.
