Startup fundraising guide

How much startup capital should you raise?

There is no single funding amount that fits every startup. A useful target is based on what your business needs to reach the next meaningful milestone, how long that should take and what risks could change the estimate.

1. Choose the milestone first

Define what success should look like at the end of the funding period. Examples include launching a tested product, completing a pilot, buying initial inventory or proving that a customer acquisition channel works. Make the milestone specific enough to evaluate.

2. List the costs required to reach it

Include essential product or service costs, people, software, equipment, marketing, compliance, delivery and other operating expenses relevant to your business. Separate one-off setup costs from recurring costs. Do not include spending simply because it would be nice to have.

3. Estimate the time required

Map the activities to a realistic timeline and estimate how long the business needs to operate before reaching the milestone. If revenue is expected during that period, use conservative assumptions and explain the evidence behind them. Do not count uncertain sales as guaranteed cash.

4. Add a sensible contingency

Projects can cost more or take longer than expected. Identify the risks most likely to affect your plan and estimate a reasonable buffer based on those risks. Explain the basis for the contingency rather than adding an arbitrary percentage without thought.

5. Test your assumptions

Build a base case and a more cautious case. Ask what happens if sales arrive later, costs rise or a key supplier is delayed. If the business cannot survive a modest downside, you may need to adjust the plan, reduce scope or reconsider the funding approach.

6. Explain the use of funds

A potential backer should be able to see how the requested amount connects to specific costs and milestones. A clear breakdown helps you explain why the amount is appropriate and what you will measure after receiving it.

A simple planning formula

As a starting point, estimate essential one-off costs + expected operating costs over the plan period + a justified contingency − reliable funding already available. This is a planning aid, not a substitute for detailed financial modelling or professional advice.

Avoid raising a number just because it sounds impressive

A larger target may require more time, evidence and investor interest; too small a target may leave the business short before the next milestone. Revisit the target as you learn more and keep your assumptions documented.
