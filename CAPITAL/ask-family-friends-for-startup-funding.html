Startup fundraising guide

How to ask family and friends for startup funding

Asking people you know to back your business can feel more personal than approaching a professional investor. Preparation helps you communicate clearly while protecting relationships and giving people the freedom to make their own decision.

1. Decide whether this person is appropriate to approach

Consider whether the person understands your business, has expressed interest in entrepreneurship or could comfortably discuss the opportunity. Do not assume that closeness means they can afford to invest. Never pressure someone, borrow against their essentials or treat a personal relationship as an obligation.

2. Prepare before starting the conversation

Know your target amount, what the money will pay for, the milestone it should support, what evidence you have and what risks remain. Prepare a short summary that the person can review later. If you cannot explain the request plainly, refine the plan before asking.

3. Make a respectful, specific ask

Explain the business, the amount you are seeking and the proposed arrangement. For example: “I’m building [business] for [customer group]. I’m seeking [amount] to fund [specific milestone]. Would you be open to reviewing a short summary and discussing whether this opportunity is right for you?” Adapt the wording to your real circumstances; do not make claims you cannot support.

4. Make it easy to say no

Tell the person there is no pressure and that you value the relationship regardless of their answer. Avoid repeated messages, guilt, artificial deadlines or promises of guaranteed returns. A clear no should end the funding ask unless they choose to reopen the discussion.

5. Be clear about the arrangement

A gift, a loan and an equity investment are different. They can create different legal, tax and financial consequences. Do not leave expectations vague or rely only on a verbal understanding. Seek qualified advice and use appropriate written documentation before money changes hands.

6. Follow up professionally

Ask whether they would like more information and agree on a sensible time to follow up. Record what was discussed and send only the material they requested. If they decline, thank them. You may ask whether they know someone who might be a fit, but do not make them responsible for your fundraising.

What if they cannot invest?

A person may still help by giving feedback, introducing you to a potential customer or pointing you toward a relevant programme. Ask only if they are comfortable. Your network can offer value beyond money.
