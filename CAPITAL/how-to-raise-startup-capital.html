How to raise startup capital: a practical guide

Raising money for a startup can feel confusing when you are starting from scratch. The most useful first step is not sending messages to everyone you know. It is getting clear about what the business needs, why it needs it and who may be a suitable person or organisation to approach.

1. Define what the money is for

Start with the next milestone your business needs to reach. It might be building and testing a first version, purchasing initial inventory, completing a pilot or reaching a repeatable sales target. List the costs connected to that milestone and make your assumptions explicit. A specific use of funds is easier to explain than a general request to “support the business.”

2. Work out a realistic funding target

Estimate the direct costs, essential operating expenses and a reasonable contingency. Avoid raising an arbitrary amount because another startup raised it. Your funding target should be linked to a clear plan and timeline. If the estimate is uncertain, state the assumptions and identify what you still need to validate.

3. Compare possible funding sources

Depending on the business and your circumstances, possible sources may include revenue, personal savings, grants, suitable loans, angel investors, accelerators or people in your existing network. Each route has different costs, eligibility requirements, obligations and risks. Venture capital is not suitable for every business model.

4. Prepare a concise explanation

Be ready to explain what the business does, who it serves, what evidence you have that customers want it, how much you are seeking, how the money will be used and what milestone it should help you reach. Be honest about risks and what remains unproven.

5. Build a prioritised outreach list

List people and organisations that may be relevant, note why each could be a fit, and identify a respectful way to start a conversation. Ask for advice or an introduction when a direct funding ask is not appropriate. Track contact dates, responses and follow-up actions.

6. Make the ask and follow through

Be specific about the request and the next step. Give people time to consider it, accept a no without pressure and avoid implying that returns are guaranteed. If someone is interested, document the discussion and obtain qualified legal and financial advice before agreeing to terms or accepting investment.

Common mistake: confusing activity with progress

Sending many messages is not the same as building a fundable case. Review your progress weekly: have you clarified your numbers, spoken to suitable people, learned from objections and improved your plan? Fundraising may take longer than a month, and no guide can guarantee a result.
